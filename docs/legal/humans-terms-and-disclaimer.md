## 0. What this covers and the posture it protects

HUMANS is a **general wellness and fitness app**. It estimates a motivational "Physical Age" from wearable/health data and generates a **personalized weekly training programme** (strength / cardio / intervals) plus nutrition, sleep, and step nudges. A clinical stress test found that in edge cases the programme could prescribe vigorous training (e.g. intervals) to users for whom it may be inappropriate. Engineering is shipping the minimum correctness fix (no intervals prescribed with zero intensity history); the **residual training risk** is covered here by disclaimer + Terms + provable in-app consent, in the same manner as mainstream wellness apps (Apple Fitness+, Peloton, Nike Training Club).

This document is designed to keep HUMANS clear of:
- **App Review Guideline 1.4.1** (medical apps / physical harm) — HUMANS makes no accuracy claim about a health measurement taken from device sensors and makes no diagnostic/treatment claim.
- **App Review Guideline 5.1.3** (health & health-research data) — HUMANS provides a benefit directly to the user, does not conduct human-subject research, and does not misuse HealthKit data. (Privacy DPO owns 5.1.3(i)–(ii) compliance in the privacy policy.)
- **The medical-device line** — HUMANS is **not** a regulated medical device (US FDA, EU MDR, UAE MOHAP/DHA) and must never be described as one. It offers general fitness/wellness guidance, not diagnosis, treatment, cure, or prevention of disease.

It relies on the **general-wellness safe harbor** (US FDA "General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices", 2019): a product that promotes a general state of health or a healthy activity, and does not make a disease claim, is a low-risk general-wellness product outside active device oversight. The disclaimer + consent below keep every user-facing surface inside that framing.

**Red lines — a disclaimer is NOT enough if any of these occur (escalate to CEO / me before shipping):**
- The app states or implies a **diagnosis, disease-risk score, or medical status** ("you have / are at risk of X", a "normal/abnormal" flag, a reference range).
- The app claims to **treat, cure, prevent, or mitigate a disease**, or to be **clinically proven to extend lifespan**.
- Physical Age is presented as a **biological/clinical age** or a **life-expectancy prediction** (see §D and `docs/physical-age-evidence.md`).
- The programme **acts on the user without their confirmation** in a way that could cause harm, or targets a **clinical population** (cardiac rehab, post-surgical, prenatal medical supervision, eating-disorder recovery) as such.
- Marketing positions the programme or Physical Age as **medical judgement** rather than wellness/documentation.

If a future build crosses any of these, the mitigation is a **regulatory pathway or a hard product change**, not stronger T&Cs. A disclaimer never cures a medical claim.

## 3.3 Does this posture keep us clear of App Review?

**Yes, on current framing — with conditions.** The wellness framing + the in-app "consult a doctor" reminders + no device-sensor accuracy/medical claim keep HUMANS inside the general-wellness lane and satisfy the relevant guideline expectations:
- **1.4.1** applies to medical apps and to apps making health-measurement **accuracy claims** or **diagnosis/treatment** use. HUMANS makes neither; the "remind users to check with a doctor" expectation in 1.4.1 is met by §B and the training-tab/onboarding reminders. Keep Physical Age framed as an estimate (no accuracy claim about a diagnostic measurement) and do **not** claim to measure blood pressure/glucose/oxygen from device sensors.
- **5.1.3** — HUMANS provides a benefit directly to the user and (per Privacy DPO) must disclose the specific health data collected and not use HealthKit data for use-based data mining or write false data to HealthKit. HUMANS does **not** conduct human-subject research, so 5.1.3(iii)–(iv) (participant consent + ethics-board approval) do **not** apply — do not describe any feature as "research" or a "study," which would pull us into that regime.

**App Review approval is NOT regulatory clearance.** Passing App Review says nothing about FDA/MDR/MOHAP status; the non-device posture rests on intended use and claims, not on Apple's decision. Keep the two separate in every memo and store listing.

**The red line for App Review specifically:** if HUMANS ever (a) claims to measure a vital sign from device sensors, (b) presents a diagnosis, disease-risk, or "normal/abnormal" health status, (c) claims to treat/prevent disease, or (d) labels a feature as human-subject "research," then a disclaimer + Terms are insufficient and it needs either a product change or the corresponding clearance/consent+ethics regime. Those are the same boundary events in §0 — route to me and the CEO before shipping.
